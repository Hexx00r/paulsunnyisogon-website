How I compared them

I judged all four on the five things that decide this for an AU trade business:

  • AU fit: AUD billing, SMS through local carriers, AU phone numbers, support in our time zone.
  • Total monthly cost for a whole crew: the real bill once your apprentice and office person have logins, not the entry sticker.
  • Quoting and booking workflow: how a website enquiry becomes a booked job with minimal manual handling.
  • Marketing automation: what happens to a lead who goes quiet, and who answers while you're mid-driveway.
  • Lock-in: how hard it is to walk away if it doesn't suit you.

Where a fact could drift, like an exact plan price, I'll flag it rather than invent a number. Prices change; structure doesn't.

ServiceM8 is the one I'd shortlist on Australian fit alone. Plenty of owners pair it with a website and answer calls themselves, which works fine when leads aren't the problem.

Jobber: the polished all-rounder

Jobber is a North American platform and the most polished product here: clean quoting, scheduling, invoicing and client management for home services, and many cleaners use it happily. If you want the nicest quoting experience and don't care where the company is based, Jobber earns its shortlist spot.

The catches: it bills in US dollars, so every charge converts on your card, and it's priced per seat (check current pricing; entry plans are commonly listed around {{JOBBER_PRICE_USD}}), so the bill grows with every login. Marketing automation runs thinner than GoHighLevel's: reminders and follow-ups, yes, but the instant-quote funnel and review engine aren't its focus.

Housecall Pro: great product, wrong postcode

Housecall Pro deserves fairness: it's an excellent product for American home services, strong dispatch, quoting and payments, built for how that market works. If your business operated out of Texas instead of Albury–Wodonga, it would sit alongside Jobber.

For Australia it loses on fit rather than quality. It bills in US dollars (entry pricing is commonly listed around {{HOUSECALLPRO_PRICE_USD}}; check current pricing), and its support hours and SMS plumbing are built for North America. A fine product pointing at the wrong country.

Questions pressure washers ask about CRMs

Which CRM is best for a one-truck pressure washing business in Australia?

Most one-truck operators are limited by leads and follow-up, not job paperwork, and for that GoHighLevel is the strongest fit: missed-call text back, instant quotes, automated sequences, flat pricing around $150 AUD a month for unlimited users. If job cards and the diary are the mess and the phone already rings enough, ServiceM8 is the better pick. Match the tool to the bottleneck.

Do these CRMs bill in Australian dollars?

Only ServiceM8 bills in AUD, a quiet advantage for an Australian business. GoHighLevel, Jobber and Housecall Pro all bill in US dollars, so each charge converts on your card at the day's rate. That doesn't rule them out, but the real cost drifts a few dollars either side of any converted figure.

Can I get Australian SMS and phone numbers?

On GoHighLevel, yes: you provision an Australian number and texts send through local carriers, which powers missed-call text back here. ServiceM8, being built in Australia, assumes AU numbers from the start. Jobber and Housecall Pro can message Australian customers, but their messaging plumbing is North American at core, so test delivery first.

What about local support hours?

ServiceM8 support runs in Australian business hours, which matters when your only admin time is early morning or after the kids are down. The other three are US companies, so live support largely runs in North American hours, with docs and communities filling the gap. It matters less once everything is set up.

Do I need a CRM or just a website?

A website captures interest; a CRM makes sure none of it leaks. If you get a handful of enquiries a week and reply within the hour, a website plus your phone is honestly enough. The moment enquiries slip because you're on the tools, or quotes go unchased, you need the follow-up layer a CRM provides: instant-quote website first, automation behind it.
