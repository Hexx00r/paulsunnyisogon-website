How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Pressure Washing Business (2026)

Short answer: ask at the moment the customer is happiest, which is right after the job, while the driveway still looks new. Send an SMS with your direct Google review link within the hour, automate that message with a tool like GoHighLevel, and back it up with a QR code on your trailer and flyers. Never buy, gate, or discount for reviews, and reply to every single one. Reviews are a ranking factor for the local map pack, so this is SEO work, not vanity.

Pressure washing is a trust business. A stranger with a machine that strips paint is about to work on someone's biggest asset, so before they book, they check the map pack and skim the stars. Put two operators side by side in the same suburb and the one with more recent reviews wins the call, even if the other bloke has been around longer. That matters twice: reviews influence where you rank locally, and they decide who gets the tap once you're there. A steady review stream compounds: better rank, more calls, more jobs, more reviews.

Why Google reviews move your local ranking

Google orders the local map pack on relevance, distance, and prominence, and reviews feed straight into prominence. Three things matter:

  • Count. More genuine reviews than the operators you actually compete with for your suburbs.
  • Recency. A profile collecting reviews this month looks alive; one whose last review is a year old looks dormant, whatever the total says.
  • Owner responses. An owner who replies to everything reads as a business that looks after its customers.

An operator with fewer but fresher reviews can outrank a bigger competitor who stopped asking. Reviews also win the click: at the same map position, the higher rating with a recent "great job on our driveway" review is who gets called. Meanwhile hipages will happily keep selling you shared leads at a few hundred dollars a month; reviews are the marketing asset you actually own.

Step one: grab your direct review link

In your Google Business Profile there's a short "ask for reviews" link that opens the review box in one tap: no searching your name, no scrolling. Copy it, test it on your own phone, then use the same link everywhere. The SMS, the invoice email, the QR code. Every extra step between the customer and the review box costs you reviews, and one tap is the whole game.

Step two: the review SMS that sends itself

The timing that works: job done, customer standing there admiring the house wash, text lands within the hour. Something like: "Thanks for having us today. If you're happy with how the driveway came up, a quick Google review helps us more than anything: [your link]." That's it. One message, one link. A single reminder two days later if they haven't clicked, then silence.

One Australian rule to respect: the Spam Act 2003. The practical read for owners: send review requests to customers who gave you their number to organise their job, keep the message about that job, and stop if they ask you to. Don't bolt them onto a promo list.

Step three: QR codes where eyes already are

Not everyone clicks a text; some prefer to scan. Print a QR code that opens the same review link and put it where customers already look:

  • Trailer side. Your biggest free billboard, parked in the customer's street for the whole job.
  • Flyers and door hangers, especially in the streets you're already working.
  • The invoice and any card or magnet you leave behind.

Most phones open the link straight from the camera, no app needed. It catches the "yeah, I'll do it later" customer while they're still standing there.

What never to do

  • Never buy reviews. It breaches Google's policies, it's detectable, and a suspended profile means starting from zero.
  • Never pay or discount for a review. Same policy, same risk.
  • Never gate. Don't run a private survey first and only send happy customers to Google. That's review gating, it breaches the rules, and it filters out the feedback that would actually help you.
  • Don't review yourself, and don't have staff or family do it.
  • Don't nag. One ask, one reminder. Pestering happy customers is how you turn a 5-star fan into an annoyed one.

Reply to every review, especially the bad ones

Every review gets a response. The 5-stars get a short, specific thank you that names the job, which shows future readers it's a real operator, not mates padding the count.

The bad ones matter more. Reply once, calmly: acknowledge, don't argue, state your side factually, and offer to make it right with a phone number. Then stop. Future customers read your response more closely than the complaint, and a calm reply to a 2-star review convinces more people than a page of unexplained 5-stars. Arguing in the thread, or blaming the customer in public, costs you the jobs you never hear about.

Questions owners ask about Google reviews

How many Google reviews does a pressure washing business need?

There's no magic number. Aim to pass the operators you actually compete with in the map pack for your suburbs, then keep them coming, because recency counts as much as the total. Ask every customer properly and the number takes care of itself.

Can I ask for reviews by SMS in Australia?

Yes, done sensibly. Under the Spam Act 2003, send to customers who gave you their number for their job, keep the message about that job, and stop if they ask. A review request tied to work you just did is fine; adding people to marketing lists off the back of it is where owners get into trouble.

Can I offer a discount for leaving a review?

No. Paying or discounting for reviews breaches Google's policies and can get your profile suspended, which costs you every review you've earned honestly. Ask everyone the same way and let the work speak.

What should I do about a fake or unfair review?

Don't reply in anger. Respond once, calmly and factually, invite them to call you, then report the review to Google through your profile with any evidence you have, like job records or messages. Some fakes get removed; even when they don't, readers see your calm response next to the complaint.

How long does it take to build a strong review profile?

With every customer asked by text and one reminder, it compounds month on month, and fresh reviews usually move your map pack position within weeks rather than months. The operators sitting on hundreds of reviews got there one job at a time; the only slow way is not asking.
