How missed call text back works

The mechanic is simple, which is why it works:

  1. A call rings out. You're mid-job, or you reject it with your elbow because your hands are full of hose.
  2. The platform sees it. The moment the call ends, a workflow fires.
  3. An SMS lands in about a minute. It comes from your business number and reads like a person: "Sorry I missed your call, still after a hand? Reply here and I'll get back to you shortly." You can add a link to book a time or get an instant quote.
  4. The caller becomes a contact. Their number is saved, tagged as a missed call, and the whole conversation sits in one inbox, not scattered across your personal phone.
  5. Follow-up runs itself. If they reply, you pick it up when you're free. If they go quiet, a polite sequence chases the job for you.

The point of the first text isn't to close the job. It's to stop the caller dialling the next tradie while you're still up the roof. A text that lands in sixty seconds does that; it's follow-up that never forgets and never waits until smoko.

Why speed wins the job

Think about how a customer behaves when their gutter is dumping water into the ceiling. They Google, they open three tabs, they ring the first two or three names. The first tradie to respond with something useful, a reply, a booking link, a ballpark quote, gets the conversation. Everyone else is hoping for a callback nobody's waiting for.

Speed matters even more in the hours you never answer: 8pm on a Tuesday, Sunday morning. Without text back, those calls ring out and the lead is gone before you knew it existed. With it, the enquiry gets answered at 8:01pm by a text offering a next step, like trying your instant quote calculator, and the lead is in your inbox next morning. The job usually goes to whoever responds first, and the only way to respond first every time is to automate the first response.

The Spam Act 2003 part, in plain English

Australia's Spam Act 2003 covers commercial SMS, so it's worth two minutes to get this right. In the normal missed call setup, the caller dialled your number first. Responding to their missed call with a single text about their enquiry sits under consent the Act calls "inferred": they initiated contact with you, and your reply relates to exactly that. You're on solid ground. Three habits keep you there:

  • Identify yourself. The text should make clear it's your business, not a mystery number.
  • Stay on the enquiry. Reply about their job. Don't pivot the same thread into unrelated promos.
  • Honour the stop. If they reply "no" or "stop", the sequence ends immediately, no exceptions.

You can't harvest every missed caller into a marketing list and blast them months later; that kind of message needs its own opt-in. This is general information, not legal advice: ACMA publishes the plain-English rules.

What it won't do

Honest limits, because this feature gets oversold:

  • It's not a receptionist. It opens the conversation and captures the lead. You still quote, book, and do the job. If texts sit unanswered for half a day, the lead cools anyway.
  • It needs somewhere to send people. A text saying "grab a time here" only works if "here" exists: a booking page or an instant-quote calculator. The text back is one gear inside a bigger machine.
  • It's only as good as your number. If your Google Business Profile and website send plenty of calls, text back multiplies their value. If the phone barely rings, fix that first.

How fast does the text actually go out?

In practice, about a minute after the call rings out. The moment your phone stops ringing, the platform fires a workflow that sends your pre-written SMS. You control the message, so it reads like you, not like a robot: an apology, a question about what they needed, and a link to book or get a quote.

Is texting back a missed call legal under the Spam Act 2003?

In the normal setup, yes. The person dialled your number, so the Act treats a single reply text about their enquiry as consent inferred from their own conduct. What you must do is identify your business in the message and stop texting if they ask.
