How to Start a Pressure Washing Business in Australia (2026): Licences, Costs and Your First 10 Customers

Short answer: you can start a pressure washing business in Australia with an ABN (free), public liability insurance, and a starter rig. There is usually no single national licence, but wastewater and council rules differ by state, so check yours before the first job. The gear is the big outlay at $700–$1,200, and your first 10 customers come from people who already know you, your Google Business Profile, and local Facebook groups.

Set up the business properly

None of this is exciting, but skipping it costs you jobs:

  • Get an ABN. Free, takes minutes online, and you need one to register a .com.au domain.
  • Pick a business structure. Sole trader is the simple start; a company costs more to run but separates you from the business. Ask an accountant once rather than guessing.
  • Register a business name if you trade under one. If it is not just your own name, register it with ASIC at $47 a year, or $108 for three years.
  • Get public liability insurance. Roughly $450–$850 a year for a sole trader at $5M cover, and non-negotiable: declare the pressure washing, because some cleaner policies exclude it. Property managers ask for your certificate of currency, and one damaged paint job pays for years of premiums.
  • Buy the .com.au domain. Your ABN gets you this. Even if the website comes later, own the name now.
  • Know the GST line. GST registration is compulsory at $75,000 annual turnover, an ATO figure. Below that, most startups keep it simple until growth makes it relevant.

On licences: there is generally no single national "pressure washing licence", but rules differ by state and council. Wastewater, chemicals, and roof work can each bring their own rules. Check your state regulator and council before your first paid job, and get the answer in writing.

What the equipment actually costs

The gear is where most of your starting cash goes, and where new operators buy twice. A starter rig needs five things:

  • A commercial pressure washer. Not the hardware store special. Underpowered gear turns a two-hour driveway into a five-hour driveway and burns out in a season.
  • A surface cleaner. The spinning head for driveways and concrete, and the biggest time-saver you will buy.
  • Hoses, guns, and fittings. Long enough to work a whole driveway without moving the machine, plus spare o-rings.
  • A trailer or ute setup. Tank, reel, straps, a way to carry water to jobs that have none.
  • Chemicals. House wash mix, degreaser, a concrete treatment. Buy trade quantities once you know what you actually use.

A workable starter rig lands around $700–$1,200 all up: a decent new washer, surface cleaner, first chemicals, at Bunnings and Kärcher AU prices as of September 2026. Buy the washer and surface cleaner new, the trailer second-hand, and resist shiny extras until the calendar justifies them.

Pricing psychology: how to charge without racing to the bottom

New operators almost always charge too little, out of a fear: "I'm new, so I should be cheap." Flip it. You are not competing on price; you are competing on trust, because you are working on someone's biggest asset.

Two mechanics matter. First, anchor high: when a customer gets three quotes, the high quote makes the middle one feel safe. If you always quote low, you train customers to see the job as a commodity.

Second, quote per m² for concrete or flat rate per house, whichever you can defend out loud. Per m² protects you: a 250m² driveway scales automatically instead of you guessing and undercharging.

When the calendar is full, raise your prices. A full book at old prices is a queue of people paying less than the next customer would have. Raise until the queue thins. That is the market telling you what your work is worth.

Your first 10 customers

The first 10 are not a marketing problem, they are a trust problem. Nobody knows you yet, so you borrow trust from places that already have it:

  1. Friends, family, neighbours, at mates rates. Not free. You need the habit of charging, and you need before-and-after photos and Google reviews more than the cash.
  2. Google Business Profile on day one. Free, and it is where "pressure cleaning near me" searches land. Collect reviews from job one.
  3. Local Facebook community groups. Every suburb has one. Post a before-and-after with a clear way to enquire. Be a local first, a business second.
  4. Real estate agents and property managers. They control endless end-of-lease and sale-prep cleans. Walk in with your insurance certificate and two great photos.
  5. Door-knock with before-and-after photos. Old school and it works. Show the photo of the neighbour's driveway you just did, then quote on the spot.
  6. Ask every customer for a referral and a review. Not "if you think of anyone". Say: "If your neighbour wants theirs done, I'll look after them, and a Google review helps me heaps."

The consistency wall

Here is the part the hype videos skip. The difference between a startup and a business is not the quality of your work. It is whether leads keep coming when you stop hustling.

Every channel in that first-10 list is a one-off. The Facebook post fades in a day. The agent moves agencies. The neighbour's street is done. Hustle channels are brilliant for starting and terrible for sustaining: the moment you are flat out on the tools, you stop hustling, and the phone goes quiet. I have lived that cycle.

The leads are the business. The gear is just how you fulfil demand. A business with steady leads survives a bad month; a business with great gear and patchy leads is a hobby with debt.

Questions people ask before starting a pressure washing business

Do I need a licence to pressure wash in Australia?

There is usually no single national licence, but rules differ by state, council, and by the work itself. Some councils regulate wastewater runoff, some states regulate chemical use, and roof or high-access work can trigger working-at-heights rules. Check your state regulator and local council before your first paid job, and get the answer in writing.

How much does it cost to start a pressure washing business in Australia?

The big cost is the gear: a commercial pressure washer, surface cleaner, hoses, a trailer or ute setup, and chemicals, which lands at $700–$1,200 for a starter rig. On top of that, budget $450–$850 a year for public liability, and $47 a year (or $108 for three) if you register a business name with ASIC, and your ABN is free. Start lean and upgrade as the calendar fills.

Should I register for GST straight away?

Not necessarily. GST registration is compulsory once your annual turnover reaches $75,000, an ATO figure, and voluntary below that. Most startups sit under the threshold at first, so owners keep it simple and register when growth makes it relevant. An accountant can confirm what fits.

How long until I get my first customers?

Do the unglamorous work and the first few come within weeks, not months: friends, family, neighbours, and local Facebook groups, because they already trust you. The slow part is not getting customers once, it is getting them consistently, so your Google Business Profile goes live on day one, not day ninety.

Do I need a website from day one?

Not on day one, but earlier than most people think. Your Google Business Profile and Facebook groups can carry the first stretch.
