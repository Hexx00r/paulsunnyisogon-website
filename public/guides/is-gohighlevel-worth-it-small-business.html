Is GoHighLevel Worth It for a Small Business? An Honest Tradie Review (2026)

Short answer: yes, GoHighLevel is worth it for a small Australian business if you're already getting enquiries you can't answer on the tools, quotes going out at 9pm or not at all, and leads that enquire once and vanish. At roughly $150/month on the Starter plan, one recovered job a month pays for the subscription. If you have no lead flow yet, no follow-up discipline, or you're hoping software will replace the effort of actually running a business, it isn't. Here's the honest maths, from someone running it in a family pressure cleaning business.

Most reviews of this platform are written by affiliates chasing a commission, so the answer is always yes. This one isn't. I run GoHighLevel for my family's pressure cleaning business in Albury–Wodonga (DJ Property & Cleaning is the live demo), and I build these systems for other Australian tradies, so I've seen both sides: owners where it's the best $150 they spend, and owners where it's a monthly donation to a dashboard they never open. The difference is never the software. Here's how to work out which side you'll land on.

The maths, honestly

The platform runs about $150/month AUD on Starter, less if you pay annually, and that price covers unlimited users and contacts: your apprentice, your missus doing the books, whoever, at no extra cost. SMS credits and a domain (about $20/year) sit on top. Full detail lives in my GoHighLevel pricing Australia guide.

Now hold that number against what you're already spending to get work. hipages runs $139–$649/month for shared leads, with typical spend around $400/month, and shared means three other contractors get the same lead and race you to it. One recovered pressure cleaning job, a driveway, a house wash, covers the GoHighLevel subscription for the month. A roof clean or a house-wash-and-driveway combo covers most of a year.

So the real question isn't "is $150 a lot?" It's "do I lose more than $150 a month in missed calls, slow quotes, and leads I meant to chase but forgot?" Most owners who honestly track that for a week don't love the answer.

When it IS worth it

GoHighLevel earns its keep the moment any of these sound familiar:

  • You miss calls while you're on the wand. Missed-call text-back fires within seconds: "Sorry I missed you, after a quote? Tap here." The customer who would've rung the next contractor stays yours.
  • Quotes go out late or never. An instant-quote calculator on your website gives a ballpark in about 30 seconds, at 8pm on a Sunday, while you're halfway through a house wash. No software can rinse a deck, but it can quote one.
  • Leads enquire once and disappear. Automated follow-up chases every quote on a timed sequence. The lead you quoted on Tuesday and forgot by Friday gets a polite nudge without you remembering anything.
  • You pay for hipages leads. Those leads are shared with competitors. A lead from your own website, captured and followed up automatically, isn't shared with anyone.
  • Reviews only happen when you remember. The day a job's marked done, the review request goes out by itself. Your Google Business Profile compounds while you work, and reviews are the ranking factor you fully control.

If two or more of those stung, the subscription pays for itself. The GoHighLevel for tradies guide covers each of these features in more depth, with real workflow screenshots.

When it's NOT worth it

This is the part the affiliates skip. Three cases where you should keep your card in your pocket:

  • You have no lead flow yet. Software doesn't create enquiries, it catches them. If the phone isn't ringing, the fix is your Google Business Profile, your website, and your reviews: the things that generate demand. A funnel with no traffic is a machine with nothing fed into it.
  • You won't keep up the follow-up discipline. GoHighLevel runs on a pipeline board: new, quoted, booked, done. If you know you'll never open it, be honest with yourself before signing up. The majority of tradie accounts I've seen are dead by week two, not because the tool failed, but because nobody looked at it.
  • You expect software to replace effort. It remembers, it chases, it texts back. You still do the quotes, the jobs, and the quality control. If you're after a set-and-forget money printer, this isn't it, and nothing is.

One more: if $150 a month is the difference between making the bills and not, don't buy it yet. This is a growth tool, not a survival tool.

What the $150 doesn't include

The subscription is only one line on the invoice, and an honest review should say so:

  • Setup. The platform assumes marketing knowledge. DIY is genuinely possible if you're patient and technical; done-for-you costs more upfront but the system actually gets finished.
  • A website. The calculator, calendar, and follow-up need somewhere to live. That's the other half of the system, and it's what my pressure cleaning website design service builds.
  • SMS credits and a domain. A few dollars of credits and roughly $20/year for the domain.
  • Compliance. Under the Spam Act 2003, marketing texts need consent. Following up your own enquirers is fine; texting bought lists is not.

How the verdict looks in a real business

DJ Property & Cleaning, my family's pressure cleaning business, runs the full loop: enquiry comes in from the website, the calculator spits out a ballpark instantly, an SMS follows up if they don't book, every lead sits on one pipeline board, and the review request fires when the job's done. The practical difference isn't glamorous: an 8pm enquiry gets answered at 8:01pm, quotes stop depending on whether I remembered them, and reviews arrive without the awkward "would you mind…" conversation. Nobody's paying me to say that; it's simply what the $150 does there.

The bottom line

GoHighLevel is worth it for a small business when you're leaking work you already have: one recovered lead a month covers it, and most established operators leak more than that. It's not worth it when there's nothing to follow up yet, when you won't look at the pipeline, or when you're expecting the tool to do the work for you. Buy it to catch what you're already generating, not to generate what you aren't.
