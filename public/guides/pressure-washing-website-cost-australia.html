Pressure Washing Website Cost in Australia (2026): What You'll Actually Pay

Short answer: a pressure washing website in Australia costs anywhere from $0–$400 DIY, $400–$1,500 for a freelancer template build, $3,000–$8,000+ for an agency, or $1,500–$5,000 for a lead-generating funnel build (site + instant quote form + automated follow-up). The price matters less than what it does: a $500 site that just sits there is more expensive than a $3,000 system that books jobs while you're on the truck.

If you're getting quotes and they're all over the place, this guide breaks down what each price bracket actually includes in Australia right now, the ongoing costs nobody mentions, and how to tell whether you're buying a brochure or a job machine.

The four price brackets (real AUD figures)

OptionUpfront costOngoingWhat you get
DIY (Wix, Squarespace, Canva sites) $0–$400 $20–$45/mo + domain ~$20/yr You build it. Fine for a brand-new business validating demand. Slow to load, generic, and you pay with your weekends.
Freelancer template $400–$1,500 ~$50–$150/yr hosting + domain A clean 3–5 page site, your logo, your photos. Looks professional, but it's a digital business card: the quote form just sends an email.
Funnel build (site + instant quote + automation) $1,500–$5,000 CRM from ~$150/mo (e.g. GoHighLevel in AUD) Instant quote calculator, leads auto-dropped into a pipeline, SMS/email follow-up, review requests. Built to convert, not just exist.
Agency custom $3,000–$8,000+ $100–$500/mo retainers common Custom design, copywriting, sometimes SEO. Quality varies wildly: many tradie agencies reskin the same template.

The ongoing costs nobody puts in the quote

  • Domain: ~$15–$25/yr for a .com.au (you need an ABN to register one).
  • Hosting: $0–$300/yr. Static sites can legitimately run free; don't pay $30/mo for hosting a 5-page site.
  • CRM/automation: if you want instant quotes and follow-up SMS, budget ~$150–$460/mo AUD for the platform (GoHighLevel converts to roughly this) plus SMS costs.
  • Edits: some agencies charge $80–$150/hr for changes. Ask before you sign.
  • Your time: the DIY option's real price. 40 hours of your weekends is 40 hours of quoting you're not doing.

What actually makes a pressure washing website bring in jobs

The difference between a $500 site and a $3,000 system isn't prettier photos. It's these five things:

  • Instant ballpark pricing. "Call for a quote" loses to the competitor who gives a number in 30 seconds. A simple per-m² calculator filters tyre-kickers and captures contact details at peak intent.
  • Before/after gallery. Your best salesperson. Phone photos are fine: the transformation is what sells.
  • Suburb-level pages. "Pressure cleaning Albury" beats a generic homepage when someone searches locally. This is how you show up outside hipages.
  • Google reviews pulled in automatically. Social proof without you copying and pasting.
  • Follow-up automation. The lead who enquires at 8pm gets an SMS in 60 seconds, not a call-back tomorrow when they've already booked someone else. This alone is the difference between a website and a funnel.

Red flags when you're comparing quotes

  • No itemised breakdown: one big number with no list of what's included.
  • You don't own the domain or the site files. If you stop paying and lose everything, you were renting.
  • No mention of mobile speed. Over half your visitors are on a phone, standing in their driveway.
  • "SEO included" with no specifics. Ranking a pressure washing site means suburb pages and a Google Business Profile, not meta keywords.
  • Lock-in contracts beyond 12 months for a small business site.

So what should you spend?

Just starting out (under ~$10k/mo revenue): DIY or a cheap freelancer build is fine. Spend the difference on Google Business Profile reviews and a decent before/after photo habit.

Established and turning away work you don't want: a funnel build pays for itself fast. One extra house wash a fortnight covers the average build within a few months, and the follow-up automation wins you jobs you never knew you were losing.

Rule of thumb: if a website option doesn't include a way to capture and follow up a lead automatically, compare its price against what you're paying hipages or Airtasker every month for shared leads. Most owners are surprised which one is actually cheaper.
