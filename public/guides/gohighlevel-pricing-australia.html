GoHighLevel Pricing Australia (2026): The Real Cost in AUD

Short answer: budget about $150/month AUD for the Starter plan, which is US$97 plus the currency conversion, then add a few dollars of SMS credits and a domain at roughly $20/year. That's the whole stack for an Australian trade business: one flat price, unlimited users and contacts, no per-seat fees. Below is exactly what lands on an Aussie card each month, and why the published US pricing never tells you.

Every pricing page and software review for GoHighLevel quotes US dollars. If you've searched for what it actually costs in Australia, you've hit the same wall: the US sticker price, republished, with a shrug about conversion. This guide does the conversion once, properly, and adds every extra that lands on an Australian statement: the SMS credits, the domain, and the bits comparison sites skip.

I run this exact stack for my family's pressure cleaning business in Albury–Wodonga, so these are real numbers from a real Australian account, not a republished US price table.

Why nobody publishes the AUD figure

GoHighLevel is an American company, and it bills every account in US dollars whether you're in Texas or Townsville. The entry plan, Starter, is US$97 a month. What reaches your card depends on the exchange rate your bank applies on the day the charge lands, which is why two consecutive months can differ by a few dollars.

The planning figure is $150 AUD a month. On a normal rate that's where US$97 converts to. Don't treat it as exact to the cent; treat it as the number to budget around, and accept the small drift either side of it.

The real monthly stack for an AU tradie

Here's the full list of what a one-truck trade business actually pays, with nothing skipped:

  • Subscription: US$97 a month, converting to roughly $150 AUD.
  • SMS credits: texts send through Twilio, which connects GoHighLevel to Australian carriers. Messages cost fractions of a cent each. A busy month of quote follow-ups, review requests and booking confirmations comes to a few dollars, and you top up as you go.
  • Domain: your quote funnel lives at its own address, yourname.com.au style. Around $20 a year, which works out to a couple of dollars a month.

Add it up and the realistic total sits just over $150 AUD a month, all in. That's the number nobody publishes, and it's the one to hold against whatever you're paying for leads right now.

The annual discount

There is one legitimate way to pay less: annual billing. Commit to a year and GoHighLevel drops the effective monthly cost compared with paying month to month. The charge still lands in US dollars, so it still converts, but the base rate is lower.

The sensible order is monthly first, annual later. Run the system for a couple of months on monthly billing while you confirm it fits how you work, then switch to annual once it's proven itself. Plenty of Australian owners also time the annual payment before 30 June so it lands in the current financial year; whether that helps you is a question for your accountant.

Higher tiers exist, but you don't need them yet

GoHighLevel sells more than one tier: Starter, plus Unlimited and Pro above it. The honest position for a trade business is that Starter does everything you need: the CRM, the automations, the calendar, the review requests, all of it. Unlimited and Pro exist for agencies running multiple client accounts under one login and for heavy senders doing serious volume, and they're priced accordingly. If you ever outgrow Starter, something has gone very right.

Unlimited users and contacts: the quiet killer feature

The pricing detail that matters most is the one US reviews breeze past. Most CRMs charge per seat: every login is a line item, so adding your apprentice, your office person, or your missus doing the books on Friday pushes the bill up. GoHighLevel doesn't work that way. Starter includes unlimited users and unlimited contacts.

For a trade business this changes the maths completely. The whole crew can sit on the same pipeline, see the same leads, and get the same reminders at one flat price. When you compare it against any per-seat CRM, compare the cost of putting your whole crew on, not just one login.
